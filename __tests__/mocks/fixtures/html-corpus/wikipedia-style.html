Lighthouse of Alexandria

The Lighthouse of Alexandria, sometimes called the Pharos of Alexandria, was a lighthouse built by the Ptolemaic Kingdom during the reign of Ptolemy II Philadelphus.[1] It has been estimated to have been at least 100 metres in overall height. One of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, for many centuries it was one of the tallest man-made structures in the world.[2]

The lighthouse was severely damaged by three earthquakes between 956 and 1323 and became an abandoned ruin. It was the third-longest surviving ancient wonder, after the Mausoleum at Halicarnassus and the extant Great Pyramid of Giza.[3]

Origin[edit]

Pharos was a small island on the western edge of the Nile Delta. In 332 BC Alexander the Great founded the city of Alexandria on an isthmus opposite Pharos. Alexandria and Pharos were later connected by a mole spanning more than 1,200 metres, called the Heptastadion.[4]

Construction[edit]

The lighthouse was constructed in the 3rd century BC. After Alexander the Great died, Ptolemy I Soter announced himself king in 305 BC and commissioned its construction shortly thereafter. The building was finished during the reign of his son.[5] It took 12 years to complete, and served as a prototype for all later lighthouses in the world.

The lighthouse was built in three stages, all sloping slightly inward: the lowest was square, the middle octagonal, and the top cylindrical. A broad spiral ramp led to the top, where a fire burned at night.
